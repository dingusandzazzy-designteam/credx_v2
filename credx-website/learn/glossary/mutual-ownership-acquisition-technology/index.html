Mutual Ownership Acquisition Technology (MoaT)

The principle that all three parties to a transaction — the customer, the business and the lender — hold a stake in what it produces.

What the term means.

Mutual Ownership Acquisition Technology (MoaT) describes how a transaction can be arranged so that no single party collects everything it produces. In the ordinary arrangement, a sale generates value in several forms at once — a fee, a customer relationship, a record of what was bought — and those forms separate immediately. The fee leaves. The relationship attaches to whoever issued the credit. The record accumulates somewhere the business cannot reach. MoaT is the name for arranging the same transaction so that each of the three parties to it holds a stake in the result.

The three parties are specific, and the term does not work without all three. The customer brings the demand and, in exchange, sees value return in a form they can use. The business generates the value and keeps the relationship the sale created, along with access to the record of it. The community lender funds the credit, carries the credit risk, and is paid for doing so. None of those roles is new. What MoaT names is the insistence that all three are stakeholders in one outcome rather than a chain in which each link extracts and passes on.

It matters most where the value is largest and least visible. Every card sale carries interchange, and on a million dollars of monthly volume that is roughly $36,000 leaving a business through a competing credit facility. Interchange recovery — the share of that fee which returns instead of leaving — runs from 40% up to 85% depending on qualifying volume. That is the arithmetic MoaT is a description of: the same transaction, the same customer, the same purchase, with the value routed to the parties who produced it rather than past them.

The word "ownership" in the expansion is doing precise work and is easy to over-read. It means holding a stake in what a transaction produces. It does not mean owning the customer, and on the data specifically the site is deliberate: what a business gets is access to the transaction record, on explicit consumer opt-in and under PIPEDA — not possession of it. The distinction is not a legal nicety. It is the difference between a business that can act on what it knows and a business that has been sold something it cannot use.

Read the acronym carefully: it is MoaT, not the English word. The casing is the client's own decision, and it exists because readers skim the four letters into the Berkshire sense of an economic moat — a defensive wall around one company. That is close to the opposite of what the term means. A moat protects one party. MoaT describes three parties holding a stake in the same thing.

MoaT appears on two pages of this site, and it is read differently on each — on purpose, because it is doing two different jobs.

On The Movement it is the philosophy: the reason the company exists in the shape it does, and the principle a business is agreeing with when it joins. Related terms.

Balance Offset & Organic Spending Trigger (BoosT)

The mechanism that applies value-back against a customer's balance at the moment of the transaction, rather than as a future discount. Where MoaT names the principle, BoosT names the action.

Questions.

What does MoaT stand for?

Mutual Ownership Acquisition Technology. The expansion is written out on first mention wherever the term appears, because the acronym on its own reads as the word moat and invites the wrong association.

Who holds a stake under MoaT?

Three parties, in every transaction. The customer, who brings the demand. The business, which generates the value. The community lender, which funds the credit. Each one holds a stake in what the transaction produces rather than one party collecting it.

Does MoaT change who carries the credit risk?

No. The lender funds the credit and carries the credit risk. The business extends credit at its own checkout without taking that risk onto its balance sheet, which is what makes the arrangement workable for a business that is not a lender.
