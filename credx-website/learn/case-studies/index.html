Case Study · Golf & Private Clubs

A Four-Club Golf Group, and $333K to $895K in Annual Value

The group runs four clubs across Western Canada and absorbs a card network cost on every dollar its members spend. This is where that money goes, what an embedded value layer returns instead, and what the group gets that it has never had before.

Embedded Value Platform & white-label member revolving credit · All figures estimated from publicly available volume data

Reducing the cost of every payment.

$300K – $714K leaving the portfolio each year in card network costs, across four clubs
~$447K midpoint annual fee exposure

Estimated from publicly available volume data at a blended 1.5% to 2.5% card network cost.

The portfolio absorbs between 1.5 and 2.5% in card network costs on every dollar it processes. On the group's own volume that is somewhere between $300,000 and $714,000 a year leaving four clubs and not coming back. It is a recurring cost with no asset at the end of it.

CredX runs alongside the existing infrastructure as an add-on. Nothing is replaced and no system is switched off. Members are directed toward the embedded value layer at checkout, which routes their spend off the card networks, and the cost reduction scales directly with how many of them adopt it.

The group's name. CredX infrastructure.

The entire platform is presented under the group's own brand, or any name it chooses for it. Members interact with a product that looks and communicates like a club offering. The technology, the compliance and the credit infrastructure are CredX, running in the background. The member relationship and the brand equity stay with the group.

Branded member revolving credit

A branded digital revolving line under the group's name, available across every touchpoint: food and beverage, pro shop, lessons, events, guest fees and tournament entry. It lives in the member's digital wallet alongside their other payment methods and no physical card is issued. From the member's side it is a club benefit. Approval, servicing and compliance all run behind it.

Flexible membership access

The option to finance membership fees monthly at 11% per annum. The club's fee structure stays exactly as it is, with no discounting and no restructuring, and the credit risk stays with CredX. It removes the largest single barrier to new membership sales and renewals without touching pricing operations.

Interest rate: 11% per annum, competitive with prime-linked personal credit.

Golf revenue here is compressed into roughly five months. Every dollar captured in season matters more.

Making the season count more.

The portfolio operates in one of Canada's most seasonally compressed markets. The majority of annual revenue, across food and beverage, pro shop, events, lessons and guest fees, is generated between late April and early October, and the shoulder months produce a fraction of peak volume.

That concentration means any uplift in per-visit spend during the season has an outsized effect on the year. A member who spends 20% more per visit in July contributes more than one who visits twice as often in November.

Revolving credit suits that dynamic specifically. Members arrive in season with a pre-approved facility already in place, so the spontaneous decisions convert instead of deferring: the extra round, the pro shop upgrade, the post-round dinner. The window is short, and capturing more of it per member is where the portfolio upside sits.

Club by club, stream by stream.

Streams 2 and 3 are entirely separate from the transaction fee savings above. Every dollar in them is incremental: it exists independently of the interchange reduction and accrues in addition to it. Conservative figures assume moderate revolving credit usage; upside reflects strong in-season member engagement.

Stream 1 · Member adoption savings

Interchange eliminated per club at 85% member adoption, estimated from public volume data.

Stream 2 · In-club revolving credit

Costs avoided on credit-funded member spend across food and beverage, retail, events and instruction, at 11% per annum. Above the transaction savings, not part of them.

Stream 3 · Member spending intelligence

Every transaction produces structured data: what was bought, at which club, at what point in the season, by which member tier. It is returned to the group as readable operational intelligence rather than raw numbers: which club peaks in July, which segments drive food and beverage volume, where spend drops off. It is visibility the group currently has no clean way to get across four clubs, and it informs staffing, inventory, event timing and membership pricing.
