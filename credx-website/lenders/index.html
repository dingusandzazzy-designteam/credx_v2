A Merchant-Linked Asset Class for Community Lenders

CredX originates consumer credit at the point of purchase and places it with Canadian credit unions and community lenders. The credit is tied to a merchant relationship the borrower already has, and repayment support is structured into the transaction rather than added afterwards.

Talk to Us

What the asset is.

Credit is extended to a consumer at the moment of a purchase, through a merchant that has an existing relationship with that customer. The lender holds the receivable. CredX originates, services the merchant relationship, and supplies the transaction data the lending decision is made on.

The borrower is not anonymous to the merchant, and the merchant has a commercial interest in the relationship continuing. That is the difference from unsecured consumer credit written at arm's length.

Instrument
Merchant-linked consumer credit, originated at the point of purchase.
Held by
The lending partner. CredX originates and services; the receivable sits with the lender.
Underwriting input
Transaction data from the originating merchant relationship, on explicit consumer opt-in.
Repayment support
Structured into the same transaction flow that created the credit. Designed to reduce default rather than to price for it.
Geography
Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Atlantic Canada.
Lending partners
Credit unions and community lenders across the regions above.

How repayment is supported.

Repayment support is built into the same transaction flow that created the credit. Because the merchant relationship continues, the structure is designed to reduce default rather than to price for it.

Value that stays in the region that produced it.

The lending is placed with institutions in the communities where the transactions happen. Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Atlantic Canada.

Regulatory posture.

CredX operates under PIPEDA, with explicit consumer opt-in on any data used in a lending decision. Lending partners hold their own regulatory obligations, and the structure is built to sit inside them rather than around them.
